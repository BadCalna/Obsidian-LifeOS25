> [!important] ## Topic
> SMART Methodology
## S.M.A.R.T
- S - Specific
	- simple, significant, and focused on a single topic or a few closely related ideas.
> [!example]  Example
>  ❌"Are kids getting enough exercise everyday?"
>  ✅"What percentage of kids achieve recommended 60 minutes of physical activity at least five days a week?"
- M - Measurable
	- Can be quantified and assessed
> [!example]  Example
>  ❌"Why did our recent video go viral?"
>  ✅"How many times was our video shared on social channels the first week it was posted?"
- A - Action-oriented
	- Encourage change
	  How to understand this? **Problem solving finds out how to turn the current situation to the ideal one, Action-oriented question revealing the path to the turnning.**
> [!example]  Example
>  ❌"How can we get customer to recycle our product packaging?" -- **This not aim for the answer that clearing next action**.
>  ✅"What design feature will make our packaging easier to recycle?"
- R - Relevant
	- Have significance to the problem you are trying to solve
> [!example]  Example
>  ❌"Why does it matter that Pine Barrens tree frogs started disappearing?”
>  ✅“What environmental factors changed in Durham,North Carolina, between 1983 and 2004 that could cause Pine Barrens tree frogs to disappear from the Sandhills Regions?

>[!example] ## Ineffective&Bad question example
- Leading questions: lead you to a certain answer
	- **You can't make interviewee share their own points of view.**
	- eg. These sandwich are the best ever, aren't they? 
- Closed-ended questions: Can simply answered by 'Yes' or 'No'
	- **This kind of  question cannot get rarely insights.
- Too vague and lack context: Just like those MBTI question

- Ensure Fairness when asking(ref. [[10.4.1 Data Fairness]])
